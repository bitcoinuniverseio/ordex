Protocol 1.2 · Provenance

Facts about collections, signed by their creators.

The registry establishes cryptographic facts about who created a collection and who belongs to it. Curation stays curation, cryptography stays cryptography, and no page ever merges the two into one reassuring badge.

The manifest

A manifest carries the network, the protocol family, a stable collection id, display name and description, the creator address, the member identity type, the member list, the membership Merkle root, metadata and trait schema hashes, media digests, a supply statement, an optional rights declaration, and the creator signature. The rights field is a creator-signed declaration, shown as one; it is never treated as a legal guarantee and never enforced by custody.

Two rules make the document tamper evident: the member list is stored sorted and duplicate-free, and the digest is SHA-256 over the sorted-key JSON of everything except the signature and the digest itself. Editing one character after signing, even the display name, breaks the digest.

Proofs that work offline

Membership leaves are domain separated, so a member proof can never be replayed as an interior node or as any other artifact's leaf. Given the manifest, a member identity, and the sibling path, anyone can recompute the root with nothing but a SHA-256 implementation: no network call, no gateway, no trust in the host. A stranger's identity, a tampered sibling, and a member removed by a newer version all refuse with MEMBER_NOT_PROVEN.

Immutable, versioned, revocable

Publication is immutable. Corrections are a new version whose digest names the manifest it supersedes. Supersession and revocation are creator-signed: a revocation binds the exact manifest digest, states a reason, and is refused if signed by any address other than the creator. History is never deleted; superseded and revoked versions stay visible with their statuses, so nothing quietly disappears.

Labels, not a badge

Separate claims, separately shown.

What the creator signed, what is anchored on chain, what the gateway curates, what third parties list, and what the community reports are five different labels and always displayed separately. A page that shows one generic badge is not showing provenance.

Verification states are precise for the same reason: creator-signed, anchor pending, anchored, superseded, revoked, and the failure states, each naming exactly what happened. An anchor conflict or a reorg never changes the fact that the creator signed, and a valid signature never claims an anchor that is not there.

Older collection roots

Existing 1.1 collection roots, the ones offers already use, stay readable through a deterministic adapter that maps them into offer verification without claiming manifest-level properties the older root never carried. Nothing breaks, and nothing pretends.

What the server cannot do

The gateway stores documents. Signatures decide what is true.

No administrator can silently rewrite a creator-signed manifest: the membership root and the digest recompute from content, and the signature address must be the creator address. The registry can refuse to serve, never rewrite.

Verify it yourself

  • Download a manifest and a proof bundle, then verify the proof offline with the SDK or verifier/collection-manifest.js.
  • Recompute the membership root and the document digest from the member list and the content.
  • Compare behavior against conformance/collection-manifest-vectors.json, including the revocation and stranger cases.

The full contract is spec/collection-manifest.md.
