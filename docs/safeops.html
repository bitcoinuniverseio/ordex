Protocol 1.2 · SafeOps

Many operations. One plan. Your signatures.

SafeOps turns a pile of small wallet chores into one executable operation: the desk composes the plan, you review its digest, your wallet signs every input, and the desk re-checks the whole world one more time before each signature and again before anything is sent.

What SafeOps executes

One plan describes one logical operation, and one signature flow covers it. The desk speaks every protocol Ordex indexes, so a single afternoon of housekeeping does not turn into fifty wallet prompts.

BATCH

Batch sends and transfers

Cardinal sends to many recipients, and ordinal or rune transfers that carry each asset to the output that receives its first sat.

TIDY

Consolidations and postage

Merge dusty outputs, prepare split and postage inputs for future inscriptions, and recover padding you no longer need.

REPAIR

RBF and CPFP

Replace a stuck transaction under the fee and safety rules below, or lift a parent with a child, computed from the combined package fee rate.

Inventory before plans

Before a plan exists, every candidate outpoint is resolved into one inventory: Bitcoin Core amount and script, confirmation state, inscription ids and satpoints, rare sat ranges, rune allocations, Counterparty attachments, and any claim the desk does not recognize. The rule that drives all of it is fail closed: an outpoint whose inventory was never examined is refused with INVENTORY_UNEXAMINED. It is never assumed cardinal.

  • An unrecognized claim fails the whole plan (UNKNOWN_CLAIM_FAILS_CLOSED). Resolve the claim first.
  • An operation declared as cardinal only refuses any input that carries a tracked asset (ASSET_IN_CARDINAL_OPERATION).
  • A rune transfer refuses inputs without a rune allocation (RUNE_INPUT_MISSING_ALLOCATION).
  • Authorities that disagree, stale data, or an indexer behind its accepted checkpoint fail the resolution closed.

This is the same registry the rest of Ordex reads, so an output carrying a Counterparty attachment is never treated as spendable change. See Counterparty heritage for how attachments are declared.

The plan you review

A plan carries its schema and protocol version, the network, the operation kind, the chain checkpoint it was built against, an expiry height, every selected input with its complete resolved inventory, a deterministic output map naming recipient, change, and preserve roles, one asset transition per tracked asset pointing at an exact output, the fee with a permitted maximum, the signing policy, human readable findings, and the digest.

The digest is SHA-256 over the sorted-key JSON of everything except the findings and the digest itself. What you review is what the digest covers: if any amount, destination, input, or fee changes, the digest changes, and the desk says so instead of adapting quietly.

The sat-flow picture

Every delivery question in Ordex reduces to one rule: an asset rides the input's sats, and it lands in the first output whose accumulated value passes that input's range start. The desk draws this as a picture before you sign anything: each input renders as a contiguous range of sats flowing left to right through the outputs in order, and the output that absorbs each range start is highlighted as the destination for anything riding that input.

The same rule that decides inscription delivery everywhere else in Ordex decides rune and attachment delivery here, so no plan can send an asset to the fee region, to unrelated change, or to the wrong recipient. If the picture and the promise ever disagree, the verifier refuses with TRANSITION_SAT_FLOW_MISMATCH.

The execution shield

Immediately before every signature, and again before broadcast, the desk refreshes the world: every input from Bitcoin Core, protocol ownership and satpoints, mempool spends and replacements, and fee estimates. If anything changed, the previous signing session is invalidated instead of silently adapted. The exact change is displayed, and the plan is rebuilt with a new digest for you to review again.

A purchase never signs over a conflict.

A marketplace purchase that would sign over a conflicting spend is blocked with a precise reason before your wallet even opens. The shield runs per signature, not once at the start.

Partitioning at scale

One logical operation may carry up to 500 asset transfers or 1,000 cardinal recipients. When a single transaction would exceed weight, input, output, ancestor, descendant, or node policy limits, the desk partitions the operation into deterministic standard transactions before the first signature. Every generated transaction, dependency, fee, and recipient is shown first.

An operation is never silently split after signatures have begun. If the desk must divide your work, you see the whole division before your wallet is asked for anything.

Replacement and acceleration

Replace by fee is offered only when node policy accepts the replacement and you control every input it requires. The replacement preserves every asset-bearing output, every third-party payment, and every amount that may not change. The incremental fee comes only from your own cardinal change or newly added inputs you own. You see the old fee, the new fee, the increment, both effective fee rates, and every changed input or output.

Child pays for parent is offered only when you control a spendable output whose spending moves no tracked asset. The child fee is computed from the combined parent-and-child package fee rate under Bitcoin Core package, ancestor, descendant, dust, and standardness policy. An asset-bearing output may fund a child only when the child provably preserves the complete asset inventory.

After the broadcast

Relaying a signed transaction happens only after your explicit broadcast action. From that moment the operation is monitored for mempool admission, conflicts, replacement, inclusion in a block, drop, and reorg. Each transition appends an ordex-event/v1 envelope you can consume through the realtime developer network.

A reorg produces an explicit reverted event naming the event it reverses, and the operation returns to a recoverable terminal state, never to an ambiguity. You always know whether your operation is pending, gone, or done.

What your wallet signs

The desk composes. You sign. You send.

The gateway never signs, never contributes funds, and never broadcasts on its own initiative. Your wallet signs every input of every transaction in the plan, exactly as the plan describes, and nothing reaches the network until you take the broadcast step yourself.

Because the shield re-reads the world before each signature, what your wallet is shown at signing time is the same state the plan was built against, or the session stops and a fresh plan is presented instead.

Verify it yourself

Every signed result is checked again after your wallet returns: the plan digest must match, the inputs and outputs must match the plan in identity, order, script, and value, the fee must match, and signatures may exist only at the indexes the signing policy requires, under the approved sighash type. You do not have to take the desk's word for any of it.

  • Recompute the plan digest yourself from the sorted-key JSON, as the spec requires.
  • Run the reference verifier at verifier/safeops.js over any plan and signed result.
  • Compare behavior against conformance/safeops-vectors.json, including the inventory, sat-flow, and refusal cases.
  • The SDK exposes the same checks; see the developer network for the client.

The full contract, including every refusal code, is spec/safeops.md. To sign the plan from an air-gapped device, read cold signing and watch-only.
